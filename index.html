Solving the equation, one variable at a time

Trust=Flexibility−Friction+Transparency+Control

Two needs. Two products.

I designed two paths: time before payment with Pay in 30 Days, and predictable installments with Ratenzahlung.

Why did flexibility
require three pages?

I consolidated three application pages into one by aligning with my checkout partner to surface installment options earlier and streamline terms behind links.

What am I agreeing to?

Customers saw every option and its estimated cost inside checkout, with the full terms before commitment.

What if I need more time?

Customers could reschedule after reviewing the new date and fee—adding control, and a new revenue stream.

The outcome.

Four variables, four decisions. Together they changed what customers were willing to do.

PayPal credit portfolio · US + UK 02 · Options → Strategic choice → Repeatable system

One core flow. Controlled variants.

Options considered

Option 01Framework-Aligned Experience MigrationTrade-offFastest and lowest effort, but preserves existing UX issues and may create future redesign work.
Option 02End-to-End Checkout TransformationTrade-offGreatest UX improvement potential, but requires the most time and effort and carries the highest launch risk.
ChosenOption 03Validated Pattern-Led OptimizationTrade-offBalances effort, speed, and risk, but may limit product-specific optimization or carry Pay in 4’s constraints into other flows.

WhyOne common journey with room for real product and regulatory differences.

Before · Legacy Pay in 4

Three application steps stood between the offer and the purchase.

Choosing the product dropped customers into a separate application, then returned them to a checkout they had already left.

After · Redesigned Pay in 4

One page brought the decision back into checkout.

Offer context, customer details, autopay and the agreements collapse into a single review-and-apply page, and the approval returns to the purchase in progress.

Ms. Sunshine · Preschool operations 03 · Options → Strategic choice → Repeatable system

Capture the day once. Use it twice.

Options considered

Option 01Batch reports at the end of the dayTrade-offMinimizes classroom disruption, but relies on recall and delays parent visibility.
Option 02Generate reports from passive signalsTrade-offReduces teacher input, but increases system complexity and lowers confidence in accuracy and context.
ChosenOption 03One activity record powers bothTrade-offRequires lightweight, real-time capture, but eliminates duplicate work and creates immediate parent value.

WhyRemoved duplicate work while creating immediate parent value.

Activity recorded

Log once by a teacher

Start from a child or from an activity, add details, a photo, or a note, and log it for everyone who took part.
